---
title: Boot timeline
description: Replay a boot's journal evidence on an interactive timeline.
---

Boot Timeline replays the journal evidence a Linux host retained for one boot. Choose the current boot or one of up to ten recent boots, press **Play**, and recorded events appear at the times the journal observed them.

The replay never reboots or changes the host. It is a reconstruction of retained evidence, not a recording of the entire boot: the journal may have rotated, kept nothing, or been only partially readable, and every such state is labeled instead of silently shown as empty.

The replay window covers the first 2 minutes of the boot by default. Choose 30 seconds, 1, 2, 5, or 10 minutes, or pick **Custom…** for any length from 10 seconds to 30 minutes. Play back at 1×, 2×, 5×, or 10× (or half speed); changing speed never re-reads the host. Use the slider or click the timeline to seek, and **Previous notable event** / **Next notable event** to jump between failures, warnings, and milestones.

The timeline shows three lanes at most — milestones, unit events, and warnings or failures — and only the lanes that have evidence. Click a marker to see its timestamp, source, unit, the raw journal message, and how many similar events a cluster stands for. **Show loaded events** opens a searchable list of everything loaded in the window, with **Load more** when the journal still has retained pages.

**Current-boot diagnostics** below the timeline keeps the contemporaneous reads for the running boot: total, kernel, and userspace timing, the slowest units, and current failed units. These describe the system now, so they are not part of historical replays.

Nothing here is saved: replay evidence stays in memory for the session only, and the connecting account is never elevated without the one-time sudo retry it asks for.
