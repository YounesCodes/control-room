---
title: Systemd
description: Find services and other systemd units on a Linux host.
---

Systemd lists the host's loaded system services, timers, mounts, and sockets. Rows show the unit name, description, and state. No unit is selected initially; select one to inspect it or enable **Relationships**. Refresh keeps your choice. Failed units appear first; inactive and disabled units are not failures.

**Focused** starts with services, timers, sockets, and path watchers, hiding routine systemd, D-Bus, login-session, and module-loader units. Failed units of every type remain visible. Choose **All units** beside it to include system services, mounts, devices, targets, and every other collected type. The count shows visible units out of the total when they differ. Search and specific type filters include the full inventory. This choice stays with the workspace; dependency graphs always include all reported unit types.

Select the failed count to find failures. **Find units** expands search inside the toolbar. Its X clears a query; with an empty field, it hides search. **Filter and sort units** opens state, type, and sort controls below its icon. The heading tooltip shows all counts and collection time for the collected system scope.

Select a row or graph node to open its inspector. In the inventory, use **Up** and **Down** to switch units and update the inspector, following the current filters and sort order. These keys also work when the inspector itself has focus. It shows essential runtime details for the unit type, or failure evidence for a failed unit. Expand **Dependencies**, **Recent journal**, or **Advanced** when needed. Close the inspector with **Close details** or **Escape**. Missing or unsupported values appear as **Unavailable**.

**Advanced** contains enablement, conditions, assertions, and further runtime details. Its **Configuration** section reads unit files and drop-ins; **Effective properties** reads the manager's current properties. These can differ from files awaiting a daemon reload. Both reads happen on demand.

## Explore dependencies

Choose **Relationships** beside **Explorer** to open a dependency graph with up to six neighbors. The action names its target, such as **Relationships · nginx.service**. The root label names the unit being graphed. The inventory count stays the same in both views. Inventory row clicks only toggle the inspector. The graph inspector stays closed until you select a node. Drag to pan, use the wheel or zoom buttons, and choose **Fit to view** to see the full diagram. Double-click a graph node, or press **Shift+Enter** on a focused node, to refocus. Use its **Expand** button to read another neighborhood, or **Show more** to reveal more known neighbors.

Choose one relationship category:

- **Dependencies** shows Requires and Wants. They pull in other units.
- **Ordering** shows After and Before. Arrows point from the earlier unit to the later unit; ordering alone does not pull in a unit.
- **Activation** shows Triggers, such as a socket activating a service.

Relationships in the same direction share a connector. The bottom-left key explains arrow direction. Dependencies also shows line samples: solid means Requires is present, dashed means Wants only. Ordering and Activation use solid arrows. Hover or focus an edge to read its reported evidence, including combined Requires and Wants. Reverse dependencies come from systemd's properties; unit names alone never establish a relationship.

**Find dependencies** searches known relationships and keeps paths to matches. Selecting a node zooms directly to that unit and opens its inspector. Click the selected node again to close it and fit the visible graph, or use **Close details** or **Escape**. The legend and zoom controls stay in a separate footer beneath the graph. Show more, Show less, Undo, and branch expansion or collapse smoothly fit the visible graph, leaving room for an open inspector. Pan or zoom to take control; pending reads will not override your view or selected-node focus until the next expansion action. **Fit to view** restores the overview. The graph shows at most 25 units and allows 24 expanded neighborhoods. Search or refocus to explore beyond that limit.

Use **Show less** to reduce the visible set, or **Undo** to reverse the last expansion change, including a collapse. Expanded nodes keep a visible **Collapse** button. Collapsing a branch removes its deeper units unless another expanded path still reaches them. Undo history stays with the workspace and resets when you refocus, search, or change relationship category.

## Investigate a failure

Select the failed count, then a unit. **Failure evidence** shows the systemd result and recorded exit status or signal. **Recent journal** reads up to 40 recent entries visible to your SSH account. **View journal** opens the full Logs view for that unit. Journal entries and related units are evidence, not a diagnosis.

Dependency rows open the related unit in the graph. **Ports** and **Boot** are under **Advanced**. Ports shows endpoints with an explicit link to the unit; its unit label returns to Systemd. Boot diagnostics links current failed and slow units back to Systemd. Selection, filters, and graph expansion survive switching views, workspace navigation, and refreshes. A filtered selection offers a way to reveal it.

Systemd reads system units with `systemctl` and `journalctl`; it does not include user units or change service state. Details and evidence show permission errors separately. Evidence reads are limited to 128 KiB, with truncation reported. Refresh retries failed reads and keeps saved evidence visible when available.

## Windows services

On Windows hosts, the navigation shows **Services**. Search or filter the service inventory by state, then select a service to see its name, status, startup mode, and process ID. **Refresh** reads it again. This view does not start or stop services or open Windows event logs.
